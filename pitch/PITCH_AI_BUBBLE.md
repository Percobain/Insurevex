# InsureVEX pitch: "Bubble or too big to fail?"

A 2-minute spoken pitch, three 1–2 minute product videos (PROTECT, SAVE, EARN) and a 1-minute results segment.
Every number has a source at the bottom. Read **Before you record** first: three things in it change what you can say.

---

## Part 1 · The spoken pitch (about 2 minutes)

> **[0:00] Hook**
>
> Half the people in this room think AI is a bubble. The other half think it's too big to fail.
>
> Here's the thing: both halves are nervous. The believers are sitting on huge gains in a few AI stocks and can't sleep. The sceptics are sitting on cash, earning almost nothing, scared to get in.
>
> We don't need to know who's right. We built a way for both of them to win.

> **[0:20] The idea**
>
> InsureVEX connects three kinds of people.
>
> **PROTECT** is for the believer who owns the stock. They pay a small weekly fee, and if their stock crashes, they get paid. Any stock, on any exchange: Nasdaq, NSE, Tokyo, London.
>
> **SAVE** is for the cautious money: family offices, high-net-worth investors, DAO and crypto treasuries holding millions in stablecoins. They want their money back, guaranteed, plus more than government bonds pay.
>
> **EARN** is for people who believe fear is overpriced. They put money in the pool that pays for crashes, and they collect the fees.

> **[0:45] How the money moves**
>
> When a saver puts in $1,000 for three years, most of it, about $890 at today's rates, buys government bonds that grow back to exactly $1,000. That's the guarantee. It never touches the stock market.
>
> The small part left over, plus the EARN money, backs the crash protection. The fees the believers pay become the savers' extra.
>
> **The bulls insure the bubble. The cautious get paid for it.** And if the crash comes, the EARN money pays first. The savers' $1,000 is still sitting in government bonds.

> **[1:10] Does it work?**
>
> We tested it on 25 years of real prices, 543 stocks, 23 stock markets. Through 2008, through COVID: no saver ever got back less than they put in. The pool earned 8.8% a year, and that's tested on years the model had never seen.
>
> Then we ran it for real on Hedera: $15 billion of savings, $6.5 billion of stock protected across 9 countries, 109 on-chain transactions. We replayed the worst week in 21 years, the COVID crash. $223 million was paid to stock owners. Savers lost zero.

> **[1:40] Why on-chain**
>
> Every payout is locked away the moment protection is sold, so nobody can promise money they don't have. The fee, the protection and the payout live on one public ledger, so anyone can check them. A stock owner in Mumbai gets paid in digital dollars minutes after the market closes, not weeks later.

> **[1:55] Close**
>
> Bubble or too big to fail? Doesn't matter. Believers stay invested and sleep at night. Savers get their money back plus more. And the people who think fear is overpriced get paid to prove it.
>
> Let me show you each one.

*About 330 words. At a calm pace that's 2 minutes. To cut to 90 seconds, drop the "Why on-chain" paragraph.*

## Tough questions: short answers

**"Isn't this a CDS / a CDO? Isn't this 2008 again?"**
"It has layers like a CDO, but it has none of what caused 2008. There are no loans. Savers' main money sits in government bonds, outside the risky pool. You can only protect stock you own. Every payout is locked away upfront. Nobody borrows, so there's no chain reaction of forced selling. The one 2008 risk that applies is everything crashing at once, and we replayed exactly that on-chain."

**"What if interest rates go to zero?"**
"Then the government bond costs almost the full amount, so there's very little left over to earn extra. The saver gets roughly what bonds pay, but still gets their money back. Our test includes the zero-rate years (2009–2021). No saver went below 100%; the worst one-year plan earned +0.04%. Low rates make it less exciting, never unsafe."

**"If AI crashes, does everything blow up?"**
"No single stock can be more than 5% of the pool and no country more than 25%. Half the savers' pool money is never used. Stocks already in free fall are refused. In the worst week we replayed, the risk-takers paid; the savers didn't."

**"Why would anyone take the EARN seat?"**
"Because people overpay for fear. Over 25 years, about 74 cents of every dollar in fees went back out as payouts. The rest is the risk-taker's reward for absorbing the bad weeks."

**"Who decides the price?"**
"A pricing model tested on 25 years, signed per quote. Today there's one pricing server; the contract already supports several, and the plan is several independent pricers with the best price winning."

## Before you record (read this)

1. **Lock the interest rate, or change one sentence.** Today's contracts size every plan at a fixed 4% and hold the bonds in a vault whose rate can change later. The research locks each plan's rate on its start date, like buying a bond that ends the same day. Until the v4 contract does that on-chain, say "*in production, each plan buys government bonds that mature on the plan's end date*" rather than implying the testnet vault already does.
2. **Set the risk-taker fee to 60% on the live pools.** The tested EARN numbers (+15.7% average year) assume risk-takers get 60% of the savers' share of fees on top of their own (`proFeeBps = 6000`, as in the stress suite). The original deployments use 40%; at 40% the same test gives risk-takers about +1% a year for all that risk. Run `setRiskParams` on each chain before recording EARN.
3. **Think twice about EARN as the retail product.** The first-loss seat is the riskiest one (worst year −61%), and judges or regulators may ask why retail gets the riskiest seat while big money gets the guarantee. A safer version: retail EARN deposits into the main pool layer, which sits *behind* the risk-takers (8.8%/yr, worst year −11% in the research), and the first-loss seat is for professionals. Decide before recording, because the EARN numbers change.
4. **Check NVIDIA is quotable on the day.** If it's refused, use the refusal on camera (script above), or record PROTECT with a calmer stock and use NVIDIA only for the DeepSeek card.
5. **Say "test dollars" once.** The $15 billion run used test dollars on Hedera testnet, with each "week" lasting a few minutes and real historical moves replayed. `/admin` says this on the page; say it once out loud too, so nobody feels tricked.
6. **Survivorship.** The research uses today's large companies, so real returns would likely be a bit lower. Have the line ready: "treat these as upper bounds."
