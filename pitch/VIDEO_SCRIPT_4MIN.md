## Q&A one-liners

- **"Where does principal protection actually come from?"** Treasury bills bought on day one, sized to repay every dollar at the end date. The risky part is only the remainder, and Pro LPs sit in front of it. On testnet the Treasury custody is simulated; in production the bills mature on the plan's end date.
- **"Why does each side need the others?"** Savers need fee-payers to earn more than bonds. Stock owners need capital to back their payouts. That capital is only safe because Pro LPs take the first loss and the principal sits in Treasury bills.
- **"Isn't retail taking the first loss?"** By choice: it's the insurer's seat, with the worst year shown up front. SAVE is for money with a mandate not to lose.
- **"What if rates go to zero?"** The bonds cost almost the full amount, so the working slice is tiny, but savers still get every dollar back. The test includes 2009–2021.
- **"Isn't this 2008 again?"** No loans, no borrowing, no forced selling. You can only insure stock you own, and every payout is locked upfront.
